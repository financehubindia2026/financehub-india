When you hear that a company is “going public,” it often means it is launching an IPO. Many investors in India get excited about IPOs because they offer an opportunity to invest in companies at an early stage of listing in the stock market. But before investing, it is important to understand what an IPO really is, how it works, and how you can apply for one safely. This guide explains IPOs in simple terms for beginners in 2026.

💡
Did You Know?

SIP assets now represent about 1 in every 5 rupees held across the entire Indian mutual fund industry.

Source: AMFI Monthly Data, March 2026

What Is IPO?

IPO stands for:

Initial Public Offering

It is the process through which a private company offers its shares to the public for the first time.

Simple Meaning

An IPO is when a private company becomes a publicly traded company.

Why Companies Launch IPO

Companies go public for several reasons:

1. Raise Capital

To fund:

  • Expansion
  • New projects
  • Debt repayment

2. Increase Brand Visibility

Public listing improves credibility.

3. Provide Exit to Early Investors

Early investors can sell their shares.

4. Liquidity

Shares can be easily traded in the market.

How IPO Works in India

In India, IPOs are regulated and listed on:

  • NSE
  • BSE

IPO Process Steps

Step 1: Company Files DRHP

Draft Red Herring Prospectus is submitted to regulators.

It includes:

  • Financial details
  • Business model
  • Risks

Step 2: Approval

Regulators review and approve IPO.

Step 3: Price Band Is Set

Company decides:

  • Minimum price
  • Maximum price

Step 4: IPO Opens for Subscription

Investors can apply for shares.

Step 5: Allotment

Shares are allotted based on demand.

Step 6: Listing

Shares are listed on stock exchanges.

Types of IPO Investors

1. Retail Investors

Individual investors applying for IPO shares.

2. Qualified Institutional Buyers (QIB)

Mutual funds, banks, insurance companies.

3. High Net Worth Individuals (HNI)

Large individual investors.

How to Invest in IPO Step by Step

Step 1: Open Demat Account

Required to hold shares electronically.

Step 2: Choose Broker

Use apps like:

  • Zerodha
  • Groww
  • Upstox

Step 3: Find IPO Section

Go to IPO section in trading app.

Step 4: Enter Application Details

  • Number of lots
  • Price (or cut-off price)

Step 5: Apply Using UPI

Payment is blocked until allotment.

Step 6: Wait for Allotment

If selected, shares are credited.

Example of IPO Investment

Company IPO Price: ₹100 per share

Lot Size: 10 shares

Investment Required: ₹1,000 per lot

If allotted: Shares appear in Demat account.

What Happens After IPO Listing?

After listing:

  • Share price starts fluctuating ✔ You can sell or hold shares ✔ Market demand decides price

Benefits of Investing in IPO

1. Early Entry Opportunity

Invest before public trading begins.

2. Potential Listing Gains

Price may rise on listing day.

3. Access to Growing Companies

Opportunity to invest in expanding businesses.

Risks of IPO Investing

1. No Guaranteed Listing Gains

Price may fall after listing.

2. Valuation Risk

IPO may be overpriced.

3. Market Volatility

Price fluctuates after listing.

4. Allocation Risk

High demand may lead to no allotment.

IPO vs Secondary Market

FeatureIPOSecondary Market
StageFirst listingOngoing trading
PriceFixed bandMarket-driven
AccessLimitedOpen daily
RiskHigher uncertaintyBased on performance

How IPO Allotment Works

IPO shares are allotted based on demand.

If oversubscribed:

  • Lottery system is used (for retail investors)

Example of Oversubscription

If 1 lakh shares available but 10 lakh applications: Only 1 in 10 investors may get allotment.

Common IPO Mistakes

1. Investing Based on Hype

Avoid blindly following trends.

2. Ignoring Financials

Always check company fundamentals.

3. Expecting Guaranteed Profit

IPO listing gains are not guaranteed.

4. Investing Entire Savings

Start small and diversify.

Tips for Beginners

  • Study company DRHP ✔ Check financial history ✔ Invest only surplus money ✔ Avoid emotional decisions ✔ Think long term
