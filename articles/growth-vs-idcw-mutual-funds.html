Should you choose the Growth Option or the IDCW Option?

Many beginners misunderstand IDCW and assume it provides extra returns. Others believe Growth plans are always superior.

The truth is that both options serve different purposes and are suitable for different investor needs.

Understanding the difference can help investors align their investments with their financial goals and cash-flow requirements.

This guide explains Growth and IDCW options in detail, including how they work, benefits, risks, and common misconceptions.

What Is the Growth Option?

Under the Growth Option, profits generated by the mutual fund remain invested within the scheme.

Instead of distributing earnings to investors, the fund reinvests them.

As a result:

NAV grows over time: assuming the fund performs well.

Example

Investment:

  • ₹1 lakh
  • Growth Option
  • Fund earns returns.
  • Profits remain invested.
  • Over time, the NAV increases due to compounding.

Key Feature

  • No regular payouts are made.
  • Investors generally realize gains when units are redeemed.

What Is the IDCW Option?

IDCW stands for:

Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal

This term replaced the older “Dividend Option” terminology.

Under the IDCW option, the mutual fund may distribute a portion of accumulated gains or capital to investors when distributions are declared.

Important Point

IDCW payments are not guaranteed.

Fund houses decide:

  • Whether to distribute
  • When to distribute
  • How much to distribute

Example

Investment:

  • ₹1 lakh
  • IDCW Option
  • Fund declares a distribution.
  • Investor receives cash payout.

The fund’s NAV typically reduces by approximately the amount distributed.

How Growth Option Works

When a mutual fund earns:

  • Capital gains
  • Dividend income
  • Interest income
  • those gains remain inside the fund.
  • The profits are reinvested automatically.

Example

Initial Investment: ₹1 lakh

Annual Return: 10%

Year 1: ₹1,10,000

Year 2: Returns generated on ₹1,10,000

Year 3: Returns generated on accumulated value

This demonstrates:

Compounding

How IDCW Option Works

In the IDCW option: The fund may periodically distribute money to investors.

Example

Investment: ₹1 lakh

Distribution Declared: ₹5,000

Investor Receives: ₹5,000

NAV Reduction:

Approximately ₹5,000 worth of value is removed from the fund.

Total wealth does not automatically increase because of the payout.

Why IDCW Is Often Misunderstood

Many investors mistakenly believe:

IDCW = Extra Income

This is incorrect.

The distribution typically comes from the investor’s own accumulated fund value.

Example

Before Distribution: NAV = ₹50

After Distribution:

  • NAV may fall to around ₹45
  • The payout is not free money.
  • It comes from the fund’s assets.

Power of Compounding in Growth Option

  • Growth plans allow profits to remain invested.
  • This creates a compounding effect.

Example

Investment: ₹5 lakh

Investment Horizon: 20 years

Under Growth: Returns continue compounding.

Under IDCW:

  • Periodic distributions may reduce the amount available for compounding.
  • Over long periods, the difference can become significant.

Benefits of Growth Option

1. Maximum Compounding Potential

  • The biggest advantage.
  • Profits remain invested.

2. Simplicity

No need to track distributions.

3. Long-Term Wealth Creation

Suitable for investors focused on corpus building.

4. Goal-Oriented Investing

Useful for:

  • Retirement
  • Child education
  • Financial independence

5. Higher Growth Potential

Compared to frequent distributions.

Benefits of IDCW Option

1. Potential Cash Flow

Useful for investors seeking periodic payouts.

2. Psychological Comfort

Some investors prefer receiving periodic income.

3. Income-Oriented Planning

Can support cash-flow requirements.

4. Flexibility

Investors may use payouts for expenses or reinvest elsewhere.

Drawbacks of Growth Option

No Regular Income

Investors needing periodic cash flow may find Growth plans less suitable.

Requires Redemption for Cash: Investors generally access money through redemptions.

Drawbacks of IDCW Option

Reduced Compounding: Distributions reduce invested capital.

Uncertain Payouts: Distributions are not guaranteed.

Lower Long-Term Corpus Potential: Compared with uninterrupted compounding.

Growth vs IDCW: Example

Assume:

Investment: ₹10 lakh

Investment Horizon: 15 years

Fund Return: 12% annually

Growth Option

  • Returns remain invested.
  • Potential corpus grows through compounding.

IDCW Option

Periodic distributions may reduce reinvestment potential.

Final corpus may be lower.

Actual outcomes vary depending on payout frequency and market performance.

Who Should Choose Growth Option?

Growth may suit investors who:

  • Want long-term wealth creation
  • Do not need immediate income
  • Understand compounding
  • Invest through SIPs
  • Have long-term financial goals

Common Goals

  • Retirement planning
  • Child education
  • Wealth accumulation
  • Financial freedom

Who Should Choose IDCW Option?

IDCW may suit investors who:

  • Need periodic cash flow
  • Prefer receiving distributions
  • Have income-oriented objectives

Common Examples

  • Retirees
  • Investors seeking supplemental income

Growth Option for SIP Investors

Most SIP investors focus on long-term wealth creation.

For this reason: Growth plans are often preferred.

Example

Monthly SIP: ₹5,000

Duration:

  • 20 years
  • Growth plans allow compounding to work continuously.

Growth vs IDCW for Retirement

Early Career Investor

Age: 25

Goal: Retirement

Potential Preference: Growth

Retired Investor

Age: 65

Goal: Cash flow

Potential Preference:

  • IDCW
  • Individual circumstances matter.

Taxation Considerations

  • Tax treatment depends on prevailing laws and regulations.
  • Investors should review current tax provisions before investing.
  • Tax rules can change over time.

Common Myths

Myth 1: IDCW Gives Extra Returns

Reality: Distributions generally come from the fund’s assets.

Myth 2: Growth Plans Never Generate Income

Reality: Investors can generate cash flow through redemptions.

Myth 3: IDCW Is Guaranteed

Reality: Distributions are not guaranteed.

Myth 4: Growth Option Is Always Better

Reality: Suitability depends on investor objectives.

Myth 5: NAV Drop After IDCW Means Loss

Reality: The reduction reflects the distributed amount.

Common Investor Mistakes

Choosing IDCW Without Understanding It: Always understand how distributions affect NAV.

Ignoring Financial Goals: Investment options should match objectives.

Focusing Only on Cash Payouts: Consider total wealth creation.

Underestimating Compounding

Compounding is a major advantage of Growth plans.

Choosing Based on Marketing Terms: Evaluate actual suitability.

Growth vs IDCW Checklist

Choose Growth if:

  • You seek long-term wealth creation
  • You do not need regular payouts
  • You want maximum compounding
  • You invest through SIPs

Choose IDCW if:

  • You need periodic income
  • You prefer receiving distributions
  • Your financial plan requires cash flow
