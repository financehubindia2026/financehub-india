<p class="lead">If you’ve ever researched mutual funds, you’ve likely come across the term NAV, short for Net Asset Value. Many beginners mistakenly believe that a mutual fund with a lower NAV is cheaper or better than one with a higher NAV. Others assume that a high NAV means the fund has become expensive. In reality, NAV does not work like a stock price. Understanding NAV is important because it determines: The value of your mutual fund units The number of units you receive when investing The amount you receive when redeeming investments This guide explains how NAV works, how it is calculated, why it changes, and the common myths investors should avoid.</p>

## What Is NAV?

NAV stands for:

### Net Asset Value

It represents the per-unit value of a mutual fund.

In simple terms:

NAV tells you the value of one mutual fund unit at the end of a trading day.

### Example

If a mutual fund has:

-   Total assets worth ₹100 crore
-   Total liabilities worth ₹2 crore
-   10 crore units outstanding

NAV would be: ₹9.80 per unit

## Why NAV Matters

NAV determines:

### Purchase Price

The number of units you receive when investing.

### Redemption Value

The amount you receive when selling units.

**Portfolio Valuation:** The daily value of your investment.

## How Is NAV Calculated?

The formula is:

## NAV = (Total Assets – Total Liabilities) ÷ Total Outstanding Units

### Components of NAV

### Total Assets

Include:

-   Stocks
-   Bonds
-   Cash
-   Interest income
-   Dividends receivable

### Total Liabilities

Include:

-   Fund expenses
-   Management fees
-   Administrative costs
-   Other obligations

### Outstanding Units

Total mutual fund units held by investors.

### NAV Calculation Example

Assume:

Total Assets: ₹500 crore

Liabilities: ₹10 crore

Outstanding Units: 20 crore

### NAV Calculation

-   (₹500 crore – ₹10 crore) ÷ 20 crore
-   \= ₹24.50
-   NAV = ₹24.50

## How NAV Changes Daily

-   Mutual fund portfolios contain market-linked securities.
-   As prices change, NAV changes.

### Example

If stocks held by the fund increase in value: NAV generally rises.

### Example

If stock prices decline: NAV generally falls.

NAV is typically calculated after market hours on business days.

## How Investors Buy Units Using NAV

When you invest in a mutual fund, your investment amount is converted into units.

### Example

Investment Amount: ₹10,000

NAV: ₹20

Units Allocated: 500 Units

### Example with Different NAV

Investment Amount: ₹10,000

NAV: ₹50

Units Allocated:

-   200 Units
-   The investment value remains ₹10,000 in both cases.

## How Redemption Works

When you sell mutual fund units:

## Redemption Value = Units × NAV

### Example

Units Held: 1,000

Current NAV: ₹45

Redemption Value:

-   ₹45,000
-   Subject to applicable taxes and exit loads.

## Does Higher NAV Mean a Better Fund?

No.

This is one of the biggest misconceptions in mutual fund investing.

### Example

Fund A NAV: ₹20

Fund B NAV: ₹200

Higher NAV does not automatically mean:

<ul class="check-list good"><li><span class="ico"></span><span>Better performance</span></li><li><span class="ico"></span><span>Lower future returns</span></li><li><span class="ico"></span><span>More expensive investment</span></li></ul>

NAV simply reflects the per-unit value of the fund.

## Why Comparing NAV Alone Is Misleading

Imagine two investors.

### Investor A

Invests ₹10,000

NAV: ₹10

Receives: 1,000 units

### Investor B

Invests ₹10,000

NAV: ₹100

Receives: 100 units

Both investors have invested:

-   ₹10,000
-   Neither has an inherent advantage.

## Mutual Fund NAV vs Stock Price

-   Many beginners compare NAV to stock prices.
-   However, they are different.

## Why Old Funds Often Have Higher NAVs

Older funds may have:

-   Longer growth history
-   Compounded gains
-   Appreciation over time

### Example

Fund launched in 2005

Starting NAV: ₹10

Current NAV:

-   ₹250
-   High NAV often reflects growth over time.
-   Not necessarily better future prospects.

## What Is a New Fund Offer (NFO)?

An NFO is the initial launch of a mutual fund.

Most NFOs are offered at:

### ₹10 NAV

## Common Misconception

Many investors assume:

-   ₹10 NAV = Cheap
-   ₹100 NAV = Expensive

Reality: The NAV itself does not indicate value.

Fund quality depends on:

-   Portfolio
-   Strategy
-   Risk profile
-   Management
-   Objectives

## Should You Prefer Low NAV Funds?

Not necessarily.

### Example

Fund A NAV: ₹15

Annual Return: 8%

Fund B NAV: ₹150

Annual Return:

-   12%
-   The higher NAV fund may still deliver superior growth.

## What Drives NAV Growth?

NAV typically increases when:

<ul class="check-list good"><li><span class="ico"></span><span>Stock prices rise</span></li><li><span class="ico"></span><span>Bond values increase</span></li><li><span class="ico"></span><span>Dividends are received</span></li><li><span class="ico"></span><span>Interest income accumulates</span></li></ul>

NAV may decline when:

<ul class="check-list good"><li><span class="ico"></span><span>Markets fall</span></li><li><span class="ico"></span><span>Portfolio losses occur</span></li><li><span class="ico"></span><span>Expenses reduce assets</span></li></ul>

### NAV in SIP Investments

When investing through SIPs: Units are purchased at different NAVs over time.

### Example

This process contributes to rupee cost averaging.

### Direct vs Regular Plan NAV

The same mutual fund may have:

-   Direct Plan NAV
-   Regular Plan NAV
-   Why?
-   Expense ratios differ.
-   Direct plans generally have lower expenses.

This can lead to slightly higher NAV growth over time.

## Growth Option vs IDCW Option NAV

Growth and IDCW options may have different NAVs.

### Growth Option

-   Profits remain invested.
-   NAV grows through reinvestment.

### IDCW Option

Distributions may reduce NAV when payouts occur.

## Common NAV Myths

## Myth 1: Low NAV Means Better Value

Reality: NAV alone does not indicate value.

## Myth 2: High NAV Means Expensive Fund

Reality: Investors buy units proportional to investment amount.

## Myth 3: NAV Predicts Future Returns

Reality: Future performance depends on portfolio growth.

## Myth 4: NFOs Are Better Because NAV Is ₹10

Reality: A ₹10 NAV does not make a fund superior.

## Myth 5: NAV and Stock Price Are the Same

Reality: They are calculated differently.

## Common Investor Mistakes

## Choosing Funds Based Solely on NAV

Always evaluate the fund itself.

### Avoiding High NAV Funds

High NAV may simply indicate long-term growth.

## Chasing New Funds Because NAV Is Low

Fund strategy matters more than starting NAV.

**Ignoring Costs:** Expense ratios affect long-term returns.

### Not Understanding Unit Allocation

NAV determines unit allocation, not investment quality.

## Example: NAV Does Not Affect Investment Value

Investor A:

-   Invests ₹1 lakh
-   NAV = ₹20
-   Units = 5,000

Investor B:

-   Invests ₹1 lakh
-   NAV = ₹100
-   Units = 1,000

If both funds grow by 10%:

Investor A Value: ₹1.1 lakh

Investor B Value:

-   ₹1.1 lakh
-   The percentage return matters more than NAV.
